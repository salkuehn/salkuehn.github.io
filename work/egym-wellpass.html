EGYM & Wellpass

From one segmentation study to a framework the business can run on

Most research produces a finding. This is the story of turning research into infrastructure — a throughline across three connected pieces of work: a segmentation and Jobs-to-be-Done model that reshaped strategy, an AI-powered system that makes two years of insight instantly queryable, and an in-progress framework aiming to make research language and value legible to everyone from PM to leadership.

1. The foundation — segments, JTBD & golden questions

I partnered with an external market research agency to build a segmentation model grounded in motivation psychology (SDT), personality and training behaviour. I shaped the survey design — including the questions that would later evolve into the segment-classifying "golden questions" — while the agency ran the survey, conducted the analysis, delivered the six segments, and built the predictive algorithm. I then ran JTBD research on the three core segments to understand what each genuinely valued, not just how they behaved.

The golden questions matter because they're what makes the framework usable at scale: the minimum set of questions needed to place any individual member into a segment, enabling personalised experience, messaging and marketing copy per member — something not currently possible.

Proof it stuck — the motivation team rewrote strategy around the three core segments, shifting toward empathic, low-barrier onboarding for beginner-type segments, and designing around another segment that acts as a natural recruiter, helping hesitant members take a first step by drawing them into groups and classes.

2. Scaling insight — an AI-powered research repository

Two years of research lived across Confluence and Notion — searchable only study-by-study, so cross-study patterns rarely surfaced, and every synthesis request went through a UXR manually digging through past work. Getting a useful answer took hours, sometimes fruitlessly.

I built a structured repository in NotebookLM — 33 product notebooks, roughly 200 studies, each governed by consistent metadata (study name, product, period, market, methodology, participant type, researcher) so insight was genuinely queryable, not just stored. Access was scoped per notebook, keeping confidential projects properly restricted.

Proof it works — insight retrieval went from hours to minutes, self-serve. When a team redesigning onboarding for staffless gyms pulled together everything already known, they realised no new research was needed at all — they iterated and prototyped directly from existing insight, saving research capacity for where it was genuinely needed.

3. What's next — one shared language for research, product & strategy

I'm now building this into something more ambitious: a framework and a set of AI tools that connect segment → motivation theory → problem space → KPI → JTBD → measurable outcome — so anyone, not just a researcher, can get a grounded answer. In practice, this is currently three Claude skills: one that recommends the right segment for a given project, one that checks whether a design or flow works for a specific segment's psychology, and one — more ambitious — that tries to connect the whole chain from segment to measurable outcome in a single pass.

That last one was tested and judged too complex by my manager; it's now being split into focused, single-purpose tools instead. That's a live, ongoing process, not a finished product.

The ambition — this becomes shared language across product, design, research, marketing and leadership — for how the business talks about users and builds value for them, in both B2C and B2B (fitness partner) contexts.

Reflection

Individually, each piece stands on its own. Together, they're one bet: that a UX Research function's biggest lever isn't the next study — it's building systems that make good research thinking available to everyone, long after the original study is finished.
