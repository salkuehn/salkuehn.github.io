EGYM

Finding the trust gap between physios and patients, and shipping a fix

Context

EGYM wanted to explore a new squat machine concept and needed to understand the daily realities of the people who'd recommend and use it: physiotherapists and gym trainers. The brief was both diagnostic — what are their real workflow pains? — and evaluative — is a squat machine actually desirable to this audience?

My role & approach

I ran 14 exploratory interviews: 7 physiotherapists, 7 gym trainers — a sample sized to fit the time available while still reaching enough depth for real insight, with equal representation across both roles so I could compare where their processes aligned and where they differed. The research showed the two roles' processes and challenges were largely the same — trainers simply went into less depth in their consultations than physios did, but the overall structure held across both.

The insight

The clearest, most consistent pain wasn't about exercises or equipment — it was trust and visibility. Physios relied on patients self-reporting exercise completion between sessions (a diary entry, occasionally a photo or video). This was unreliable and left physios making treatment decisions without real data on what their patients had actually done.

What changed

This reframed the opportunity for the squat machine beyond exercise desirability — which was confirmed; squatting form is foundational and something everyone needs to be able to do safely — toward a bigger question: could the machine also close the data gap between what happens in a session and what a physio can see afterwards.

Outcome — The squat machine shipped as that year's flagship Smart Strength innovation, timed to EGYM's yearly FIBO trade-fair cycle. In its first year, usage of the machine grew steadily — workouts completed on it rose roughly 24% over a six-month period, with strong week-one retention among new users.

Reflection

Alongside the pain points feeding directly into the squat machine, the interviews surfaced a wider set of UX insights into physio and trainer processes not tied to any single feature. These were recorded for future use, and later fed into a discovery session on preventative health and physio integration — research done once, used more than once.
